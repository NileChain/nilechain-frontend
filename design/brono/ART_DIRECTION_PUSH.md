# Art direction escalation — when Brono output feels flat

Use this when the generated screens are functional but lifeless. Content prompts produce correct-but-boring layouts; this prompt supplies **craft levers** instead of content.

Paste as a follow-up on the same Brono project, then regenerate the landing page.

---

## PASTE THIS

```text
Stop. The last output was competent but lifeless — it looks like a functional wireframe with color applied, not like a designed product. I want work that would be featured, not work that would be approved. Regenerate the landing page with real art direction.

Here is the craft bar: the visual confidence of Stripe, the typographic discipline of Linear, the editorial boldness of an award-winning agency site — but the subject is Egyptian agricultural trade, so it must feel like soil, trucks, weighbridges and contracts, not like another software startup. Do not copy those sites. Match their level of intentionality.

Apply these specific levers. This is where the life comes from:

1. DRAMATIC SCALE CONTRAST
The headline should be genuinely large — think 80 to 130px on desktop — set tight, with negative letter-spacing, against tiny 11-12px uppercase or small-caps labels. Right now everything sits in the same boring middle range of 16 to 40px. Big things must be BIG and small things must be SMALL, and the tension between them is the design.

2. BREAK THE GRID
Stop stacking full-width centered sections. Use an editorial, asymmetric composition: content that starts at a column and runs off the edge, elements that overlap each other's boundaries, a product card that bleeds past a photo edge, text blocks aligned to different axes in different sections. Every section should have a different compositional archetype — never three identical card rows in a row.

3. REAL IMAGERY, ART-DIRECTED
Use photography of the actual world: Egyptian farmland and irrigation canals, tomato and wheat crates, a truck at a factory gate, a weighbridge, sacks being loaded, hands inspecting produce, a signed paper contract on a desk. Color-grade them to the palette so they feel like one film, not a stock folder. Crop aggressively and unexpectedly. Never place floating glass cards on top of a blurred photo — instead let real UI overlap the photo edge with a hard border, or let the photo occupy a decisive block of the composition.

4. ONE DARK ANCHOR SECTION
The page should not be one uniform brightness. Introduce a deep, rich dark section — the trust score or the Arabic contract is the natural candidate — with light type on it, at full bleed. That single tonal shift is what makes a page feel designed rather than assembled.

5. TEXTURE INSTEAD OF GLOSS
Add subtle physical texture: fine paper grain, a whisper of film noise, a hairline rule system, printed-document warmth. This is an agricultural contract product, so it should feel tactile. No glassmorphism, no glow, no chrome bezels.

6. DATA AS GRAPHIC ELEMENTS
The numbers in this product are the story: a trust score of 78 out of 100, a 40-point weight for crop match, a 30/70 payment split, a 40-point risk threshold. Set them enormous and confident, in tabular numerals, as compositional anchors — not buried inside small cards. A giant "78" with a tiny Arabic label under it is more powerful than a widget.

7. MOTION WITH INTENT
Scroll-linked reveals with genuine stagger, one parallax layer, one continuous element such as a slow marquee of crop and governorate names, and a hover language with real physical feedback. Restrained but present. Nothing bounces for no reason.

8. TYPOGRAPHIC CRAFT IN ARABIC
Treat the Arabic display type as artwork, not as translated filler. Tight optical spacing on large headings, generous line-height in body copy at roughly 1.7, deliberate mixing of a heavy Arabic display weight with light body weight. Include a numbered section system, small labels, and hairline dividers so the page reads like an editorial layout with a point of view.

9. MICRO-DETAIL DENSITY
The difference between fine and forgettable is at the 4px level: hairline borders, precise optical alignment, considered corner radii used consistently, one accent color that appears rarely and therefore lands hard, and a spacing system that is obviously deliberate.

10. THE HONESTY RULE STILL HOLDS
No invented statistics, no fake partner logos, no trust seals we do not have, no purple or indigo gradients, no crypto imagery, no robots or AI brains, no sparkle icons, no generic "unlock the future" copy. The drama must come from typography, scale, photography, tone and composition — never from decoration.

Self-check before you deliver: if any section could be dropped into a random SaaS website without anyone noticing, that section has failed. Redo it until this page could only belong to NileChain.

Regenerate the full Arabic RTL landing page at this level now.
```

---

## If it is still flat

Push one lever at a time instead of all ten:

```text
The composition is still too safe. Regenerate only the first viewport, and this time make the headline at least 110px, break the layout into an asymmetric two-column editorial split, and let a real photograph of an Egyptian field occupy a full bleed block that the product card overlaps. Show me three distinct directions for this first viewport so I can choose.
```

Asking for **three directions of one viewport** is usually more effective than regenerating a whole page.
